## Where It Starts
DJI Pilot for Windows sits at the center of a flight, from the first checklist item to the last frame of recorded video. Pilots who fly the same kind of mission repeatedly — inspections, surveys, mapping runs — tend to use it to turn a route into a saved mission once, then fly it again with far less setup each time. The dji pilot app interface keeps telemetry, the live camera feed, and mission controls all visible at once, so nothing important is hidden behind a second window.

> Good flight software tends to disappear into the background of the job — the fewer decisions it demands mid-flight, the more attention a pilot can give to the airspace itself.

## What You'll Find Once You're In
Once you're past the first setup, these are the parts of DJI Pilot that tend to matter most in everyday flying.

- Live telemetry and video feed shown together
- Map-based waypoint mission planning
- Gimbal and payload camera control mid-flight
- A flight log you can look back on after landing

## What It Asks of Your Machine
DJI Pilot doesn't demand exotic hardware, but it does lean on your machine during a live flight: expect it to want a recent 64-bit version of Windows, a modern processor with a bit of headroom for video processing, enough memory to keep a video feed and map view running smoothly, and free storage for the installer plus whatever flight recordings you keep afterward.

## Settling It In
Getting DJI Pilot running starts with the download button above; the installer itself asks little more than a location and a moment to finish copying files. The first launch is where the real setup happens — signing into a DJI account, then pairing the app with an aircraft or controller so the two can talk to each other. From there, the app settles into the background of normal use, waiting for the next flight rather than asking for attention.

## The First Few Minutes
1. The first screen a new pilot sees is the sign-in prompt, followed shortly by a request to connect a DJI aircraft or controller.
2. Once connected, the live telemetry and video feed appear, and the app walks through a short pre-flight checklist before it allows takeoff.
3. After a flight or two, checking telemetry, confirming the checklist, and reviewing the flight log afterward becomes routine rather than something to think through each time.

> First flights tend to feel more procedural than the ones that follow — that's normal, and it usually fades within a handful of missions.

## How It's Licensed
DJI Pilot is free to download and use, offered as proprietary software by DJI rather than as an open-source project.

## If the Story Hits a Snag
Most snags get worked out inside DJI Pilot's own Help section, which walks through the setup and connection issues pilots run into most. When that's not quite enough, the official DJI Pilot website carries the rest of the documentation and support resources.
